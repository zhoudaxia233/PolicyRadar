STATEMENT
European common enforcement priorities for 2026 corporate reporting

OBJECTIVE
    The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) issues its annual Public Statement setting out
    the European common enforcement priorities (ECEP) for the 2026 annual financial reports1 of issuers
    admitted to trading on European Economic Area (EEA) regulated markets.
    ESMA, together with national enforcers in the EEA (enforcers), will pay particular attention to these areas
    when examining the application of the relevant reporting requirements. In addition, enforcers will continue
    to focus on other entity-specific issues. Based on the examinations performed, enforcers will take
    enforcement actions whenever material misstatements are identified and ESMA will subsequently report
    on their findings. In addition to these European priorities, enforcers may also set national priorities.
    ESMA underlines the issuers management and supervisory bodies’ responsibility as well as the
    importance of the oversight role of audit committees to:
        •   ensure the overall internal consistency of the annual financial report,
        •   implement and supervise internal controls, and
        •   ultimately contribute to high-quality annual financial reports.
    ESMA urges issuers, auditors and supervisory bodies to consider the topics and detailed
    recommendations included in this Public Statement when preparing, auditing and supervising the 2026
    annual financial reports. These recommendations should be taken into account in light of their materiality
    and relevance for the issuer’s operations and annual financial report.

SECTION 1: PRIORITIES RELATED TO IFRS FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
1.1 Priority 1: Geopolitical risks and uncertainties
General remarks
Geopolitical risks and uncertainties continue to be highly relevant for the 2026 financial statements of
European issuers. Ongoing geopolitical tensions, trade policies, sanctions, changes in trade flows and
regional conflicts may have significant direct and indirect implications for issuers' operations, financing
conditions, asset valuations and cash flow projections, as well as related accounting estimates, judgements
and disclosures. In this regard, ESMA highlights the messages included in 2025 ECEP Statement2.
Liquidity risk and liquidity management
In the current environment, ESMA emphasises the importance of transparent and entity-specific disclosures
regarding liquidity risk. IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures requires disclosures that enable users of
financial statements to evaluate the nature and extent of liquidity risk arising from financial liabilities3.
In particular, issuers should provide a complete and clear maturity analysis for financial liabilities (including
lease liabilities and trade payables) based on undiscounted contractual cash flows, including principal and
interest payments4. The analysis should use appropriate time bands5 that enable users to assess the timing
of expected cash outflows and changes in liquidity risk. When significant repayments are concentrated in
a particular period, issuers should carefully assess whether narrower maturity intervals may be needed.
For example, a broad category of one to five years may not be appropriate where material repayments are
expected within a specific year of that range, in which case more granular maturity intervals, including
intervals shorter than one year, may be warranted. Issuers should also explain significant changes in
liquidity risk exposures from the previous period6 and, if relevant, disclose a maturity analysis for financial
assets held for managing liquidity risk 7.
Furthermore, issuers should provide entity-specific qualitative and quantitative information on how liquidity
risk is managed and, where relevant, explain the relationship between qualitative and quantitative
disclosures about liquidity risk 8. Where issuers refer to future financing actions as part of their liquidity
management strategy, they should clearly distinguish between financing that is committed and financing
that remains subject to conditions, negotiations, approvals or other uncertainties. Where an issuer faces
significant liquidity pressures or financial difficulties, ESMA expects more granular disclosures on liquidity
risk, liquidity management measures and sources of funding.
In some circumstances, cash and cash equivalents held by issuers may not be available for use by the
consolidated group, for example, where cash balances are held in jurisdictions that are subject to exchange
controls or other legal, regulatory or contractual restrictions. Issuers should carefully assess whether such
restrictions exist and exercise appropriate judgement in determining whether cash balances are effectively
available to the group. In those cases, ESMA expects issuers to provide entity-specific disclosures on the
judgement applied, as well as the nature, amount and location of material restricted cash balances 9.
ESMA reminds issuers of the disclosure requirements concerning covenants attached to borrowing
arrangements10. Disclosures should enable users of financial statements to understand the risk that the
liabilities classified as non-current at the reporting date could become repayable within twelve months after
the reporting period. When determining the level of detail of covenant disclosures, issuers should take into
account their specific circumstances, including the risks of covenant breaches. In particular, issuers should
consider disclosing the ratios or performance measures, applicable thresholds, the frequency of testing and
the outcome of covenant assessments during the reporting period. Such disclosures are particularly
important where covenant compliance may be affected by adverse economic conditions.

Fair value measurement and disclosures
Current developments can significantly affect fair value measurements by influencing valuation
assumptions, observable inputs, and market expectations. ESMA notes that fair value measurements are
required or permitted in several areas of financial reporting 13.
Where valuation techniques based on discounted cash flow models are applied 14, issuers should
carefully assess whether current uncertainty has been adequately reflected in the valuation. With rising
inflationary pressures, volatile energy prices and an increasingly complex operating environment, the range
of reasonably possible future outcomes widens, making robust cash flow projections more
challenging. Present value techniques differ in how they adjust for risk and in the type of cash flows they
use. When applying present value techniques using risk-adjusted expected cash flows, issuers should
consider the range of possible outcomes. The discount rate adjustment technique should reflect
assumptions that are consistent with risks inherent in the cash flows.
Issuers shall maximise the use of relevant observable market inputs whenever available and minimise
the use of unobservable inputs15. Unobservable inputs shall reflect the assumptions (e.g. risk assumptions)
that market participants would use when pricing the asset or liability 16. If an issuer uses quoted market
prices as inputs to its fair value measurements and concludes there has been a significant decrease in the
volume or level of activity for the asset or liability, further analysis of the transactions or quoted prices is
needed. Reduced trading activity should not automatically result in a conclusion that a quoted price does
not represent fair value or that a transaction in that market is not orderly 17.
Issuers should provide transparent, entity-specific disclosures regarding the valuation techniques
applied, key assumptions, significant judgements and the related estimation uncertainty 18. Where fair value
measurements rely on significant unobservable inputs (Level 3), issuers should, in particular, provide19 the
disclosures on quantitative information on significant unobservable inputs20, the amount of gains or losses
attributable to changes in unrealised gains or losses relating to Level 3 measurements 21 and sensitivity
information 22 , 23 . For financial assets and financial liabilities, where reasonably possible alternative
assumptions could result in materially different valuations, sensitivity information should include the
quantitative effect of changes in assumptions.
If relevant, issuers should also explain how current economic and geopolitical developments affected the
valuation process for their Level 3 measures24. In periods of heightened uncertainty, it may be necessary
to incorporate appropriate risk adjustments where significant measurement uncertainty exists 25.

Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations are met26.

1.2 Priority 2: Restructuring provisions and debt restructuring
Restructuring provisions
Changing economic conditions may lead issuers to restructure their operations. ESMA reminds issuers
of the requirements in IAS 3730 which explain how the general recognition criteria for provisions apply to
restructuring costs. A constructive obligation arises only when an entity has developed a detailed formal
plan that creates a valid expectation among those affected that the restructuring will be carried out by
starting to implement that plan or announcing its main features to those affected by it 31. In measuring
restructuring provisions, issuers should include only those direct expenditures that are necessarily entailed
by the restructuring and are not associated with their ongoing activities 32 . Issuers should also assess
whether a restructuring affects the application of other IFRS standards (e.g. IAS 36, IFRS 5).
ESMA encourages issuers to provide transparent disclosures on the main categories of expenditures
recognised within such provisions. This would help users of financial statements understand the underlying
obligations and the expected future cash outflows. Where applicable, disclosures on restructuring
programmes should be consistent across the management report and financial statements.
ESMA highlights that the termination benefits for employees made redundant as part of the restructuring
are recognised in accordance with the specific requirements of IAS 19 Employee Benefits33. Furthermore,
where restructuring programmes affect defined benefit arrangements, issuers should assess whether
pension curtailments or settlements have occurred and account for any resulting effects under IAS 1934.
Debt restructuring
In the current macroeconomic environment, some issuers may need to renegotiate or restructure their
financing arrangements, which may involve changes to contractual terms, including interest rates,
maturity dates, repayment schedules, lenders, guarantees, collateral arrangements and covenants.
Issuers are reminded that modifications of financial liabilities should be carefully assessed under IFRS 9
Financial Instruments to determine whether derecognition is required 35 . While IFRS 9 includes the
quantitative '10% test'36, ESMA expects issuers to also consider qualitative factors, as these may indicate
that a modification is substantial (e.g., a change in the currency of denomination or the introduction of an
embedded equity component).
Issuers should provide disclosures on entity-specific accounting policies relating to the assessment of
whether modifications of financial liabilities result in their derecognition 37 , as well as the significant
judgements applied in making those assessments during the reporting period 38. ESMA notes that additional
information about modifications, including their quantitative effects on the issuer’s financial statements, may
be necessary to enable users of financial statements to understand the nature and extent of risks arising
from the affected financial instruments39.

SECTION 2: PRIORITIES RELATED TO SUSTAINABILITY STATEMENTS
Financial year (FY) 2026 is a transitional year when it comes to the reporting standards issuers use to
prepare their sustainability statements. Article 2(1) of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1563
permits issuers to prepare their sustainability statements based on one of the following: i) ESRS Set 140 as
amended by the quick-fix delegated act41, ii) ESRS Set 242 (i.e. the simplified ESRS) or iii) ESRS Set 1 as
amended by the quick-fix delegated act and with those reliefs from ESRS Set 2 which are listed in Article
2(1)(b) of the Commission Delegated Regulation.
Issuers should be transparent about the version of the ESRS they have used to prepare their
sustainability statement43. This can suitably be included in the basis for preparation of the sustainability
statement. If issuers choose to report under ESRS Set 1 and to make use of the available ESRS Set 2
reliefs, they are reminded that the cases listed in Article 2(1)(b) are exhaustive and may not be extended
further.
Within the EU Taxonomy framework, the Climate Delegated Act, the Environmental Delegated Act and
the Disclosures Delegated Act are under revision. To acknowledge this, while the considerations ESMA
outlined in previous years’ priorities continue to apply, ECEP 2026 do not include specific Taxonomy
recommendations. Nevertheless, ESMA confirms that enforcers will continue to supervise Taxonomy
disclosures in FY2026 sustainability statements.

2.1 Priority 1: Continuing the progress along the materiality learning curve
Disclosures on the materiality of impacts, risks and opportunities (IROs) and the double materiality
assessment (DMA) process remain focus points for enforcers 44, as these disclosures – and the underlying
assessments – provide the foundation for the rest of the sustainability statement.
Assessing impacts rigorously and providing clear disclosure on the DMA process
During the DMA process, ESMA encourages issuers to assess positive and negative impacts with
the same level of rigour. ESMA has observed that some issuers provide more extensive disclosure on
positive impacts than on negative impacts which may not give users a sufficiently balanced view of the
issuer. According to the qualitative characteristics, a neutral depiction shall cover favourable / positive and
unfavourable / negative aspects in a balanced way and give equal attention to negative impacts, positive
impacts, risks and opportunities 45 . As such, issuers should provide clear disclosure on any impact
materiality thresholds46 – especially when they use different thresholds for assessing the materiality of
their positive and negative impacts – and disclose their positive and negative impacts with comparable
granularity.
ESMA highlights the importance of high-quality disclosure on the identification, assessment and
determination of IROs. Importantly, such disclosure should cover both the issuer’s own operations and
its upstream and downstream value chain47.
ESMA reminds issuers that information on the DMA process is most valuable when it is concise,
company-specific and linked to the topic that is being assessed for material IROs. For instance, there
may be differences between the process for identifying and assessing IROs related to own workforce and
the process for identifying and assessing IROs related to climate change. When the DMA process for a
particular topic is significantly different from the process used for other topics, it will be helpful for users to
receive information about this.

The ESRS topics / subtopics (and sub-subtopics for ESRS Set 1) address a broad range of sustainability
matters, and for many issuers it can therefore be expected that they cover most of the material IROs. While

2.2 Priority 2: Providing connected information on IROs, PATs and metrics
ESMA expects consistency across issuers’ disclosures on IROs, policies, actions and targets
(PATs) and metrics. While this applies to all topics, for FY2026 enforcers will focus on disclosures on E1
Climate change and S1 Own workforce due to the advanced maturity of these topics.
Enhancing connectivity within the FY2026 sustainability statement
Issuers should be clear about how their policies and targets 48 address the IROs described under
SBM-3 / IRO-2. For instance, when a GHG emission reduction target does not cover the issuer’s full GHG
inventory, this should be explained 49. ESMA also encourages issuers to aim for comparable granularity
when describing their policies and their IROs. For example, if an issuer has several material S1 IROs
(discrimination, adequate wages, etc.) which are covered by one broad human rights policy, users will
benefit from an explanation of how this policy covers each of the S1 IROs.
Furthermore, ESMA reminds issuers that when they do not have PATs for a material IRO, they have to
disclose this fact50. Some issuers disclose PATs which do not address the entirety of the IRO they purport
to cover, but this fact may be masked by the aggregated disclosure of IROs. ESMA expects issuers to
improve transparency in this regard. For example, when an issuer discloses an aggregated IRO relating to
both physical and transition climate risks, but the PAT only addresses transition risks, it is important to
mention this. When an IRO spans the issuer’s own operations and value chain, but the PAT only addresses
own operations, this should be clarified51.
When an issuer discloses the key actions through which it manages its material IROs, it should explain
how these key actions help it reach corresponding policy objectives 52, for instance, by stating how
the issuer’s key social actions (e.g. training, grievance mechanisms, audit of suppliers) serve its policy
objectives (e.g. preventing discrimination, respecting human rights). ESMA also reminds issuers of the
requirement to be transparent about current and future significant financial resources (CapEx / OpEx)
dedicated to their key actions53. When an issuer plans key actions and has not assigned significant financial
resources to completing those actions, it should consider if it would be material for users to receive an
explanation for why no significant resources are needed.
ESMA reminds issuers who include a summary in their sustainability statement to make sure that the
information disclosed therein meets the qualitative characteristics. This includes a neutral depiction which,
among other, requires a balanced coverage of favourable / positive and unfavourable / negative aspects 54.
Improving connectivity between the FY2026 sustainability statement and other parts of the FY2026 AFR
ESMA has in some cases observed a mismatch between the IROs disclosed under SBM-3 and the
risks included elsewhere in the annual financial report (AFR) and reminds issuers to enhance
consistency across these two areas. Furthermore, some issuers omit information on the current
financial effects of their material IROs, and ESMA highlights that this disclosure should be provided in line
with applicable requirements to support links with the issuer’s financial reporting55.

Ensuring clarity on targets
Targets permit users to track issuers’ progress and keep them accountable. Issuers should disclose their
measurable, time-bound and outcome-oriented targets60 and use a clear terminology to define their
targets. ESMA furthermore reminds issuers to make sure that methodological information is disclosed
and specific to the target at hand. Presenting this information in a table may help, as combining
information about several targets in one paragraph is often confusing. If some of the metrics underlying a
target are not covered by the ESRS, methodological information on those metrics should be provided61.
ESMA reminds issuers of the importance of disclosing their progress towards meeting their targets, in
particular where performance has changed significantly compared to previous reporting periods. Where an
issuer has met a target it disclosed in the previous period, it should state this, and in this situation, users
will benefit from knowing whether it has established a new target. If an issuer has missed (or abandoned)
a target with the current reporting year as the target year or considers that it will not be able to meet a
target by the target year, it should disclose that fact62.
Furthermore, ESMA reminds issuers of the importance of clear disclosure on methodological
changes compared to previous years as such changes can affect assessment of the progress towards
the target. For example, if an issuer has changed its baseline year or value, this should be indicated and
explained (e.g. with reference to a new perimeter due to an acquisition, extended GHG coverage or
improved Scope 3 estimates). The issuer should provide the same transparency if it has changed its target
year or value63. Similarly, if the issuer has amended the calculation methodology of a metric used to track
progress against a target, it should be transparent about any consequences such changes may have had
on the disclosed progress towards the target.
E1 requires issuers to state whether a GHG emission reduction target is science-based and compatible
with limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees. ESMA notes that some issuers mention ‘science-based’ or
‘1.5 degrees’ without explicitly stating whether and how their targets meet these characteristics,
and this should be improved to avoid misleading users64. Unless the ESRS specify otherwise65, ESMA also
reminds issuers to disclose absolute (and not only intensity) values for their targets66 and urges them to
clearly distinguish between GHG emission reduction targets (gross) and other targets which are
netted, as mixing these types of targets risks creating an inaccurate picture of the issuer.
The topical disclosure requirements related to S1 targets are less detailed, but ESMA encourages issuers
to maintain their discipline when presenting S1 targets in line with MDR-T / GDR-T. ESMA has observed
instances where issuers do not make a clear distinction between broad commitments and actual
targets (measurable, time-bound and outcome-oriented). ESMA reminds issuers to be clear about what
are their actual targets and to link those targets to their material IROs and related policies and key actions 67.

 SECTION 4: GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS AND REMINDERS

 This section includes general considerations and reminders that are not part of this year’s ECEP. ESMA
 encourages issuers, auditors and supervisory bodies to consider them when preparing, auditing or
 supervising annual financial reports, as they may relate to future reporting periods, management report
 disclosures or past publications.

Implementation of IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements
      •   IFRS 18 becomes effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027 and
          requires comparative information for the preceding period. The new presentation and disclosure
          requirements also apply to interim financial statements prepared in accordance with IAS 34 Interim
          Financial Reporting in the year of initial application, together with extensive transitional disclosures.
          ESMA reminds issuers of the importance of ensuring high-quality implementation of the standard
          and providing transparency on its impacts. In this regard, ESMA refers to its statement on the
          implementation of IFRS 1868. ESMA urges issuers to closely monitor discussions within the IFRS
          Interpretations Committee (IFRS IC) regarding the application of IFRS 18, as its decisions provide
          further clarification on the application of certain requirements.
      •   With regard to issuers’ 2026 financial statements, issuers should, in accordance with the
          disclosure requirements of IAS 8, provide entity-specific information on the expected impact of
          IFRS 18. This may include explanations on quantitative changes to the presentation of the
          statement of profit or loss, assessments of specified main business activities and details on
          measures that are expected to meet the definition of a management-defined performance measure
          (MPM), including information on whether the issuer plans to discontinue or to introduce new
          measures in public communications, as well as other significant judgements and key accounting
          policy decisions.
      •   Issuers should also consider how the implementation of IFRS 18 may affect other areas included
          in public communication, e.g. financial targets and other metrics used in business plans. Moreover,
          issuers are reminded that IFRS 18 will have implications for ESEF reporting. Early assessment of
          the required XBRL mappings, taxonomy elements and potential extension needs (including for
          comparative information) may facilitate a smoother transition and help ensure high-quality digital
          reporting from first-time application. Issuers are reminded that the ESEF Taxonomy package
          published by ESMA in April 2026, applicable to 2026 annual financial reports, includes separate
          IAS 1 and IFRS 18 entry points to support early familiarisation, testing and implementation.
      •   Finally, in relation to performance measures based on hypothetical income and expenses included
          in regulated information documents, prospectuses and disclosures under article 17 of MAR, ESMA
          reminds issuers of Q&A 188469 on the Guidelines on Alternative Performance Measures (APMs).
          Notably, ESMA urges issuers, before making adjustments to previously used APMs or including
          new APMs, to carefully assess whether the intended adjustments or new APMs would provide
          transparent and useful information to the market, improve comparability, reliability and/or
          comprehensibility of APMs and of the financial information disclosed to the market.

Preparing for future sustainability reporting
      •   ESMA observes that many targets are approaching their deadline as the year 2030 draws
          close. In future reporting periods, it may become increasingly clear that previously set 2030 targets
          cannot be achieved. In that case, it will be important for issuers to promptly make disclosures to this
          effect in line with Priority 2 ‘Ensuring clarity on targets’.
      •   Additionally, issuers should prepare to draw up their FY2027 reporting based on ESRS Set 2 if they
          have not already done so for FY2026. One notable change in Set 2 is that undertakings should
          not report non-material information except in specific cases 70, and issuers should be ready to
          adjust their current approach to this provision.
