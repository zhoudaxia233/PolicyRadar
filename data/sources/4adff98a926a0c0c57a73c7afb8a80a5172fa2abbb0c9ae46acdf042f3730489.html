    EUROPEAN COMMISSION

    Brussels, 1.10.2026

    COM(2026) 523 final

    2026/0294(COD)

    Proposal for a

    REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

    amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 as regards the inclusion of hydrogen as a reference fuel for the testing of an engine type or engine family

    (Text with EEA relevance)

    {SWD(2026) 288 final}


    EXPLANATORY MEMORANDUM

    1.CONTEXT OF THE PROPOSAL

    •Reasons for and objectives of the proposal

    Hydrogen is an integral part of the European Union's strategy for achieving climate neutrality by 2050, as outlined in the EU Hydrogen Strategy 1 . The strategy positions hydrogen as a versatile energy carrier capable of decarbonising sectors that are challenging to electrify. By promoting the production and use of renewable and clean hydrogen, the EU aims to scale hydrogen technologies, reduce dependency on fossil fuels, and propel sustainable economic growth. This vision is further supported by the REPowerEU plan 2 , which aims to reduce dependency on fossil fuel imports and accelerate the transition to renewable energy sources, including clean hydrogen production and utilization across various sectors.

    The potential of hydrogen to facilitate decarbonisation is acknowledged within the UNECE framework, where it is considered a viable fuel option for non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) 3 . This recognition highlights hydrogen’s environmental benefits and its capacity to significantly lower emissions from NRMM engines. Despite this international acknowledgment, hydrogen is not listed as a reference fuel under Article 25 of Regulation (EU) 2016/1628. The absence of hydrogen in the regulatory framework limits the NRMM sector's ability to transition to more sustainable technologies, hindering the EU's goals of reducing carbon emissions and dependence on fossil fuels as outlined in the European Green Deal 4 . Without recognizing hydrogen as a reference fuel, the EU risks innovation and market entry for hydrogen-powered NRMM, thereby missing out on technological advancement opportunities.

    Therefore the proposal aims at:

    Closing the Regulatory Gap: The primary objective is to amend Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to include hydrogen as a reference fuel, ensuring that EU regulations are consistent with international standards and aligned with the Union's environmental and climate objectives.

    Facilitate Decarbonization and Enhance Energy Security: By incorporating hydrogen into the regulatory framework, the initiative supports the EU's strategy to reduce carbon emissions and achieve climate neutrality by 2050. It helps reduce reliance on fossil fuels and contributes to a resilient and sustainable energy system.

    Stimulating Innovation and Industrial Competitiveness: Recognising hydrogen as a viable fuel encourages innovation within the NRMM sector, supporting research, development, and deployment of hydrogen technologies. It aligns EU regulations with UNECE standards, facilitating international trade and maintaining the competitiveness of EU manufacturers globally.

    •Consistency with existing policy provisions in the policy area

    Article 35 of Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 allows for exemptions for new technologies or new concepts, thereby paving the way for innovations to be integrated within the NRMM sector. A manufacturer may apply for an EU type-approval in respect of an engine type or engine family that incorporates new technologies or new concepts. Following the exemption granted to the Netherlands under Article 35 5 , Article 36 obliges the Commission to take necessary steps to adapt the delegated and implementing acts to take account of these technological developments. The inclusion of hydrogen in the list of reference fuels is a precondition for the subsequent amendments to the delegated and implementing acts

    •Consistency with other Union policies

    The proposal is consistent with existing Union policies aimed at sustainability, climate action, and technological innovation.

    The introduction of hydrogen as a fuel for NRMM is integral to advancing the EU’s climate neutrality goals. The NRMM sector is significantly impacted by the Effort Sharing Regulation (ESR), which is the primary regulatory driver for their decarbonisation. The ESR mandates emissions reductions across non-ETS sectors, including transport, construction, agriculture, and inland navigation, facilitating compliance with EU-wide climate objectives.

    The Clean Industrial Deal 6 underscores the EU’s commitment to transforming its industrial base as part of the Green Deal. By supporting the adoption of hydrogen technologies, the amendment aligns with the objective to transition industries towards low-carbon production processes, enhancing competitiveness and fostering sustainable growth. It also supports the EU Climate Law 7 , which makes climate neutrality by 2050 legally binding and sets a 2030 emissions‑reduction target, by helping cut industrial emissions.

    The proposal is fully aligned with the EU Hydrogen Strategy 8 , which positions hydrogen as a key energy carrier in the EU’s path to climate neutrality. This strategy provides a framework for scaling up hydrogen production and utilisation across various sectors, including transport and industry, aligning with the EU’s overarching climate objectives.

    The amendment is in line with the EU Industrial Maritime Strategy 9 , where hydrogen as reference fuel for NRMM engines is specifically mentioned under Pillar IV - Access to Innovation.

    The amendment is consistent with the Regulation on CO2 emission standards for heavy duty vehicles 10 , which aim to reduce emissions from the transport sector and promote the uptake of zero and low emission vehicles, including hydrogen-powered vehicles. By enabling the use of existing technologies in the NRMM sector, the proposal ensures consistency in how decarbonisation is approached across different types of machinery and vehicles, promoting proven technology for emission reduction.

    2.LEGAL BASIS, SUBSIDIARITY AND PROPORTIONALITY

    •Legal basis

    The legal basis for the proposal is Article 114 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

    •Subsidiarity (for non-exclusive competence)

    The NRMM market involves extensive transnational trade. Without a unified EU approach, disparate national regulations could lead to market fragmentation, creating technical barriers to trade and inefficiencies for businesses operating across multiple countries.

    An EU-wide regulatory framework is essential to maintain a level playing field. This prevents fragmentation and facilitates the free movement of goods within the internal market.

    The exclusion of hydrogen from the list of possible fuels for use in NRMM could impede technological innovation and delay the adoption of hydrogen technologies, counteracting shared EU environmental goals and market integration efforts.

    •Proportionality

    The proposal aligns EU regulations with already established international standards set by the UNECE.

    The initiative does not impose additional costs or new administrative burdens on stakeholders, but it removes obstacles for the placing on the market of innovative new technologies that meet the requirements of international standards. This means any required adjustments are proportionate to the benefits of environmental sustainability and market competitiveness.

    The proposal is designed to facilitate the EU's strategic goals in climate action and technological innovation without imposing unnecessary burdens on businesses or Member States. It is focused specifically on closing the regulatory gap in a way that supports the Union’s broader decarbonization and sustainability objectives.

    The proposal maintains proportionality and ensures that regulatory changes are necessary, suitable, and balanced relative to their anticipated benefits.

    •Choice of the instrument

    Once the Netherlands have been authorised to grant EU type-approval for hydrogen engines pursuant to Article 35, the Commission shall adapt the Regulations accordingly as laid down in Article 36 of the Regulation. However, the inclusion of new reference fuels like hydrogen is not included in the empowerments granted to the Commission. Therefore, an amendment to the existing regulation is the necessary instrument to achieve this alignment, ensuring regulatory coherence and harmonisation within the internal market.

    3.RESULTS OF EX-POST EVALUATIONS, STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATIONS AND IMPACT ASSESSMENTS

    •Ex-post evaluations/fitness checks of existing legislation

    Not Applicable

    •Stakeholder consultations

    The elements of the proposal have been discussed with and supported by Member States and stakeholders at the NRMM expert group meetings in September 2024 and October 2025.

    •Collection and use of expertise

    The amendment is based on established international standards and testing expertise derived from UNECE Regulation No. 49 11 and the recent amendments to Regulation No. 96 12 for mono- and dual-fuel engines, which recognise hydrogen as a potential fuel for NRMM engines. Additionally, the process follows the Article 35 procedure of Regulation (EU) 2016/1628, as demonstrated by the authorisation granted to the Netherlands to grant EU type-approval for a hydrogen mono-fuel engine 13 . The certification tests performed by the designated technical service verified compliance with the existing environmental protection standards.

    •Impact assessment

    The proposal does not require an impact assessment as it is aligned with the existing UNECE Regulation 96, which already recognises hydrogen as a potential fuel. The proposal follows the authorisation granted by the Commission to the Netherlands under Article 35 of the Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to grant EU type-approval for a hydrogen mono-fuel engine based on extensive documentation. Article 36 of the Regulation stipulates that where the Commission authorises the granting of an exemption pursuant to Article 35, it should immediately take the steps necessary to adapt the delegated or implementing acts concerned to technological developments. Against this background no further impact assessment is necessary.

    5.OTHER ELEMENTS

    •Implementation plans and monitoring, evaluation and reporting arrangements

    Not Applicable

    •Detailed explanation of the specific provisions of the proposal

    The amendment makes two targeted changes to Regulation (EU) 2016/1628:

    It adds hydrogen to the list of “reference fuels” used in the type-approval/emissions testing framework.

    It removes a CO₂ information/reporting obligation where the engine is operated solely on hydrogen, because there is no CO₂ of fuel origin from combustion.

    REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

    amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 as regards the inclusion of hydrogen as a reference fuel for the testing of an engine type or engine family

    (Text with EEA relevance)

    THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL OF THE EUROPEAN UNION,

    Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and in particular Article 114 thereof,

    Having regard to the proposal from the European Commission,

    After transmission of the draft legislative act to the national Parliaments,

    Having regard to the opinion of the European Economic and Social Committee 14 ,

    Acting in accordance with the ordinary legislative procedure,

    Whereas:

    (1)Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 of the European Parliament and of the Council 15 establishes emission limits for air pollutants from engines installed in non-road mobile machinery.

    (2)The Commission, by Implementing Decision C(2025) 1153 final 16 of 25 February 2025 authorised the Netherlands under the exemption regime laid down in Article 35 of Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to grant EU type-approval for a type of engine for non-road mobile machinery operating solely on hydrogen fuel. After the granting of that exemption hydrogen as a reference fuel should be included in Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to ensure consistency and harmonisation across the Union.

    (3)In order to facilitate the market access and ensure the competitiveness of Union manufacturers in the global market, it is appropriate to align the Union's regulatory standards with UNECE Regulation No 96 17 , Revision 04, which includes hydrogen as reference fuel for non-road mobile machinery engines.

    (4)Engines operating solely on hydrogen do not emit carbon-dioxide of fuel origin during combustion. It is therefore appropriate to exempt manufacturers of hydrogen engines from the obligation to report CO2 emissions as part of the type-approval process, thus simplifying regulatory requirements for that technology.

    (5)Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 should therefore be amended accordingly,

    HAVE ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:

    Article 1

    Amendments to Regulation (EU) 2016/1628

    1.Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 is amended as follows:

    1.in Article 25(2), the first subparagraph, the following point is added:

    ‘(g) hydrogen.’;

    2.in Article 43, paragraph 4 is replaced by the following:

    ‘4.    Manufacturers shall make available to OEMs the value of the carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions determined during the EU type-approval process and shall instruct the OEMs to communicate that information, together with explanatory information on the test conditions, to the end-user of the non-road mobile machinery in which the engine is intended to be installed. However, that value is not required for engines operated solely on hydrogen.’

    Article 2

    Entry into force

    This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union.

    This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.

    LEGISLATIVE FINANCIAL AND DIGITAL STATEMENT

    1.FRAMEWORK OF THE PROPOSAL/INITIATIVE

    1.1.Title of the proposal/initiative

    REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

    amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 as regards the inclusion of hydrogen as a reference fuel for the testing of an engine type or engine family

    1.2.Policy area(s) concerned

    The amendment to include hydrogen as a reference fuel for non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) in Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 primarily concerns several key policy areas:

    Environmental and Climate Policy: The proposal aligns with the EU’s broader environmental goals, particularly in terms of reducing emissions and mitigating climate change impacts. It supports initiatives like the European Green Deal, aiming to transform the EU into a sustainable economy with minimal net emissions of greenhouse gases.

    Energy Policy: The inclusion of hydrogen fuel reflects the EU's strategic direction in diversifying energy sources and promoting renewable and clean energy technologies. This is consistent with the EU Hydrogen Strategy, which seeks to leverage hydrogen's potential as a versatile energy carrier.

    Industrial Policy: By fostering the adoption of hydrogen technology, the proposal supports innovation and competitiveness within the EU's NRMM industrial sector. It encourages research and development, leading to technological advancements and the creation of new market opportunities in the NRMM sector.

    Single Market Policy: The harmonization of standards across Member States is crucial for maintaining the integrity of the EU’s internal market. The proposal facilitates the free movement of goods, reduces technical trade barriers, and ensures a level playing field for manufacturers and economic operators.

    Health Policy: By reducing emissions of pollutants, such as nitrogen oxides and particulate matter, the proposal contributes to better air quality and public health outcomes, aligning with EU objectives to improve the well-being of its citizens.

    These policy areas collectively support the EU’s strategic objectives of sustainability, competitiveness, and market harmonization, reflecting an integrated approach to addressing environmental and economic challenges.

    1.3.Objective(s)

    1.3.1.General objective(s)

    The general objective of amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to include hydrogen as a reference fuel for internal combustion engines used in non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) is to close the existing regulatory gap between the current EU regulations and the international standards set by the UNECE, which already recognise hydrogen as a possible fuel option. This amendment promotes technological advancement and environmental sustainability within the NRMM sector by facilitating the transition to cleaner energy solutions. By addressing this regulatory alignment, the initiative ensures cohesion and consistency in the Union's approach to climate change, innovation, and market harmonization, thereby supporting competitive advantage for EU manufacturers in global markets.

    1.3.2.Specific objective(s)

    Specific objective No. 1: Facilitate decarbonization, environmental sustainability, and enhancing energy security

    Hydrogen is a recognised fuel in the EU’s strategy to reduce carbon emissions and achieve climate neutrality by 2050. By including hydrogen into the regulatory framework for NRMM, this initiative contributes directly to the decarbonisation of a traditionally fossil-fuel-dependent sector.

    Diversifying energy sources by including hydrogen helps reduce the EU's dependence on fossil fuels. Through initiatives like the EU Hydrogen Strategy, this move supports the development of a sustainable and secure energy system, contributing to the overall resilience of the EU's energy supply.

    Specific objective 2: Stimulating Innovation and Industrial Competitiveness

    By recognising hydrogen as a reference fuel, the amendment encourages innovation within the NRMM sector, stimulating research and development of new technologies. This initiative supports the objectives of the EU industrial strategy, which seeks to harness and advance new technological solutions.

    The initiative ensures that EU regulations are harmonised with UNECE Regulation No. 96 Revision 04, fostering an integrated approach to NRMM and motor vehicle emissions regulations. This alignment is essential for facilitating international trade and maintaining the competitiveness of EU manufacturers on the global stage.

    1.3.3.Expected result(s) and impact

    Specify the effects which the proposal/initiative should have on the beneficiaries/groups targeted.

    The proposal is expected to deliver significant benefits across multiple dimensions, supporting the EU’s goals for climate neutrality, market integration, and global leadership in clean technology.

    Specific impact 1: Environmental Impact

    The use of hydrogen helps lower emissions of greenhouse gases and pollutants, contributing to improved air quality and supporting the EU’s climate neutrality goals.

    The initiative supports the transition to cleaner energy solutions, fostering a long-term shift towards sustainable industrial practices in the NRMM sector.

    Specific impact 2: Economic Impact

    EU manufacturers and suppliers along the NRMM value chain are expected to benefit from access to a harmonised regulatory framework that aligns with international standards, enhancing competitiveness in both domestic and global markets.

    The regulation fosters technological innovation by promoting research and development of hydrogen technologies, potentially leading to new business opportunities, jobs, and growth within the green economy.

    Specific impact 3: Market and Trade Impact

    The initiative ensures that NRMM products can be seamlessly traded across EU Member States, reducing technical barriers and fostering a more integrated internal market.

    Alignment with international standards facilitates smoother access to global markets, strengthening the EU's export potential.

    Specific impact 4: Regulatory Impact

    The amendment ensures consistent application of environmental regulations across Member States, reducing disparities and simplifying compliance processes for businesses.

    It aligns with and reinforces broader EU policies, such as the European Green Deal and Hydrogen Strategy, by embedding these objectives into regulatory practice.

    Specific impact 5: Stakeholder Impact

    Manufacturers and suppliers will benefit from clear regulatory environment and harmonised NRMM emission regulations, which will enhance their competitiveness.

    Citizens will experience indirect benefits through improved air quality and public health, as reduced emissions from NRMM contribute to a cleaner and healthier environment.
