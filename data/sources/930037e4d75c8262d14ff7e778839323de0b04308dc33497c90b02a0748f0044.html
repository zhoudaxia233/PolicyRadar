    Publication of an application for registration of a name pursuant to Article 50(2), point (a), of Regulation (EU) No 1151/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council on quality schemes for agricultural products and foodstuffs

    (C/2026/5253)

    Following this publication, the authorities of a Member State or of a third country, or a natural or legal person having a legitimate interest and established or resident in a third country, may lodge, in accordance with Article 17 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1143 of the European Parliament and of the Council (1) an opposition with the Commission within three months from the date of this publication.

    SINGLE DOCUMENT

    ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’

    EU No: PGI-ES-03020

    Sent on 31.10.2023

    1.   Name(s) of PGI

    ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’

    2.   Member State or third country

    Spain

    3.   Description of the agricultural product or foodstuff

    3.1.   Type of product

    Class 1.6. Fruit, vegetables and cereals fresh or processed

    Combined nomenclature code

    —

    07 – EDIBLE VEGETABLES AND CERTAIN ROOTS AND TUBERS

    0713 – Dried leguminous vegetables, shelled, whether or not skinned or split

    0713 20 – Chickpeas (garbanzos)

    3.2.   Description of the product to which the name in (1) applies

    The product covered by this Protected Geographical Indication under the name ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ is the dry fruit of the plant species Cicer arietinum L., of the Badil and Castúo ecotypes and local varieties, also found within the Lechoso, Pedrosillano, Blanco Andaluz or Macarena and Castellano commercial types.

    Physical and morphological characteristics of dried chickpeas:

    (a)

    The chickpea must be yellowish-white to brown in colour, with intense cream predominating, with reddish and orange tones being the most common.

    (b)

    The shape of the seed must be almost spherical, with a smooth surface and without cracks or roughness and free of any defects.

    (c)

    Up to 2 % of chickpeas which do not correspond to the characteristics in (a) and (b) above may be accepted, provided that they do not compromise the general appearance.

    (d)

    The minimum size must be 5 mm, with up to 5 % of smaller chickpeas accepted.

    (e)

    The weight of 1 000 chickpeas must be at least 200 grams.

    Organoleptic characteristics (after sufficient cooking, to the point at which they are at their best for consumption):

    (a)

    Buttery and smooth endosperm.

    (b)

    Soft fine skin which sticks to the seed.

    (c)

    Soft after cooking.

    All dried chickpeas which meet the physical and morphological characteristics described above belong to the ‘Extra’ class in accordance with the classification of pulses.

    Chickpeas in the ‘Extra’ class are of superior quality and their seeds have the shape, appearance, development and colouring characteristic of the commercial type (as described in characteristics (a) and (b) of this section). They must have no defects other than very minor superficial differences (characteristic (c)), provided that these do not compromise their general appearance, quality or presentation in the packaging.

    3.3.   Feed (for products of animal origin only) and raw materials (for processed products only)

    —

    3.4.   Specific steps in production that must take place in the defined geographical area

    Owing to the clear interaction between the quality of the final product, the soil in the production area, the climate and the ecotypes and varieties used, the only specific stage of production which must be carried out in the geographical area is the growing of the chickpeas in the field.

    3.5.   Specific rules concerning slicing, grating, packaging, etc. of the product to which the registered name refers

    Dried chickpeas may be marketed for up to two and a half years after the end of harvesting, provided that the best-before date indicated on the packaging has not passed.

    It is not permitted to sell chickpeas in bulk under the present PGI.

    3.6.   Specific rules concerning labelling of the product to which the registered name refers

    Labelling is carried out in packaging plants where the identification labels or secondary labels issued by the Consejo Regulador (Regulatory Board) are affixed.

    The packages must bear the label or secondary label identifying the Protected Geographical Indication, printed with a sequential and unique number for each package, in addition to the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ name and the mandatory PGI logo.

    The unique and sequential numerical identification is linked to traceability, so that all packagers of ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ are aware of the origin of the product, quantities and supplier which supplied the product. After sorting and packaging, a quantity of finished product is obtained and labelled with the number referred to, which in turn relates to a batch of finished product, which is included in the delivery orders, so that a record is kept of the destination of the products and the quantities delivered to each of them. The secondary labels will be used to identify smaller packs containing chickpeas from batches which have already been numbered and which are divided for direct marketing to the consumer.

    Only chickpeas from packing and marketing undertakings which have passed all the quality checks at the various production stages may be placed on the market with a guarantee of origin endorsed by the label of the Consejo Regulador.

    4.   Concise definition of the geographical area

    The geographical scope of the production area of the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ Protected Geographical Indication comprises the municipalities of Valencia del Ventoso, Medina de las Torres, Calzadilla de los Barros, Fuente de Cantos and Valverde de Burguillos, all located in the south-east of the province of Badajoz, bordering each other and adjacent to Valencia del Ventoso. Packing and marketing undertakings may be located outside the production area.

    5.   Link with the geographical area

    The request to register ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ PGI is based mainly on the product’s reputation, but also on its quality and characteristics.

    Specificity of the geographical area

    (a)   Natural factors

    The soils which characterise the area are loam, clay loam and clay, with a clay content ranging from 25 % to 50 %. This gives them the most suitable characteristics for growing chickpeas, as they are deep and fertile soils with sufficient moisture retention, which is essential for growing spring chickpeas.

    According to USDA-NRCS, in addition to the common Xerochrept soils in the area covered, there is an ‘island’ of more unique soils consisting of soils of the Haploxeralf type, characterised by deep, slightly neutral pH, little organic matter and a loamy-clay-sandy texture.

    The pH of the soil ranges from 6 to 8 units and is neutral for most areas (6,5-7,5). The fact that some areas are more alkaline does not harm the crop in any way as the active limestone content of all areas is less than 5 %.

    The organic matter content of these soils is suitable for farming, with an average of around 1,8 %, making them fertile soils which also have an available phosphorus content of above 20 ppm on average, which makes the land very suitable for growing pulses (which are rather demanding in this regard).

    The high organic and mineral fertility of these soils means that farmers in the area do not need much fertiliser and it can be a profitable crop, thanks to the chickpeas’ high cationic exchange capacity which provides sufficient K, Ca and Mg to ensure that these chickpeas are of Extraordinary quality.

    From the point of view of soil health, these soils are balanced and have overall stable microbial activity.

    The climate of the area is temperate Mediterranean, although it may be considered to be transitional due to its continental features and the Atlantic influence of winds from Portugal. The average annual temperature is around 16 °C, but the temperature range is close to 40 °C. Annual rainfall ranges from 500 to 600 mm. The prevailing winds come from the Atlantic.

    These soil characteristics, together with the climate of the area, make it possible for the chickpeas of the ecotype and varieties indicated to reach their full nutritional potential, with organoleptic characteristics of softness, creaminess and finesse which make them unique.

    (b)   Historical factors

    Nowadays there are a large number of references to this product, the most famous and most prominent being the ‘Feria Gastrónomica del Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ (Gastronomy Festival of the Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso), which takes place annually in the middle of August in the town of Valencia del Ventoso. A very traditional and very famous festival in the region organised by the municipal authority of Valencia del Ventoso, it includes the gastronomy competition ‘Dia del Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ (Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso day) which has been held for more than 30 years and has a major impact at regional level.

    The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ currently enjoys great renown within the region of Extremadura, evidenced by the published books which make reference to the product. The work ‘El Garbanzo un cultivo para el siglo XXI’ [The chickpea: a crop for the twenty-first century], coordinated and managed by José del Moral de la Vega and Angel Mejías Guisado and published in 1998, makes particular mention of the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’. Also the book ‘Patrimonio cultural en la Mancomunidad Río Bodión Encuentros y Desencuentros’ [Cultural Heritage in the Río Bodión Community: Convergence and Divergence], coordinated by Carlos Moreno Amador and Aniceto Delgado Méndez and published in 2018, has an entire chapter devoted to the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’.

    Another indication of its current reputation is the application for the recognition of the ‘Feria Gastronómica del Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ as a regional tourist event submitted by the municipal authority to the Ministry of Tourism of the Autonomous Community of Extremadura, which was published in the Hoy newspaper on 7 August 2018. There are also numerous awards and references, for example the famous gastronomy awards ‘A Cuerpo de Rey’ on the COPE channel, where the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ was named as the product of the year in 2021. The Dionisio Acedo Prize for Journalism awarded to María Ángeles Fernández for the article ‘Los garbanzos mágicos de Valencia del Ventoso’ [The magical chickpeas of Valencia del Ventoso], published in issue 39 of Vivir Extremadura magazine (October/November 2012), is yet another example of said reputation.

    Further evidence of the current reputation of the chickpea are, for example:

    —

    The publication in Revista Grada of 5 August 2025 entitled ‘XII Feria Gastronómica del Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’.

    —

    The special programme aired by Canal Extremadura Televisión on 13 January 2024 ‘El Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’.

    —

    The radio programme on the Onda Cero Sur de Extremadura channel ‘La Feria del Garbanzo del Ventoso’ (Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso fair).

    —

    In an article published on 16 December 2015, the Hoy newspaper states that ‘the best chickpeas in the world are Valencia del Ventoso chickpeas’.

    —

    It was also reported on www.digitalextremadura.com on 6 October 2014 that ‘300 portions of the “Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso”’ were served at the Zafra fair.

    —

    The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ was featured in a TV report broadcast by RTVE on 10 October 2012.

    Historical references to the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ are very extensive and numerous. The first records date back to the 18th century, where the manuscript ‘Record of memorable things worthy of note in this convent of Nuestra Señora de Aguas Santas in the city of Jerez de los Caballeros’ by the guardian Fray Juan Casas in 1724, a logbook for the internal use of the convent, states that ‘The chickpeas are ordered in Valenzia del barrial (the former name of Valencia del Ventoso), upon request of the convent of Burguillos, usually around the time of San Bartolomé.’

    Specific characteristics of the product

    The specific nature of the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ compared with other chickpeas lies in the tradition and history of this product over the centuries in the production area, as well as in its characteristics as the fruit of the Cicer arietinum L. plant species, the Badil and Castúo ecotypes and local varieties, also found in the Lechoso, Pedrosillano, Castellano and Blanco Andaluz or Macarena commercial types.

    The particular characteristics of ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ are its colour, weight, buttery and smooth endosperm, and soft, fine skin which adheres to the seed.

    Causal link between the geographical area and the specific quality, the reputation or other characteristics of the product

    The link between the chickpea and the geographical area, even though founded on the product’s reputation, also derives from the characteristics of the soils in the production area, the use made of the local ecotype and varieties sown and the selection of the most suitable land. This clearly influences the quality and characteristics of the product.

    The quality of this chickpea is not achieved outside the area concerned because this is dependent on the type of soil, the geographical location, the climate and the ecotypes and varieties used. The combination of clay soils and the climate results in a product with the physical characteristics defined in point 3.2, with exceptional organoleptic characteristics. The endosperm is very buttery and fine and does not form starchy lumps after cooking for two reasons: the climate of the area, and the sufficient potassium content of the land on which it is sown.

    The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ and its festival are widely recognised and very famous in the area, in the region and at national level. Given by way of example only, below are some of the countless publications on this product in recent years:

    The news item ‘El día del “Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso” cumple 27 años’ [The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ day is 27 years old], published in the Hoy newspaper on 31 July 2015, was included in the online edition of the national newspaper ABC of 15 August 2012, marking the 25th anniversary of the feast day.

    There are many articles in the Hoy newspaper published over different years which feature the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ gastronomy competition held in Valencia del Ventoso, for example the article of 5 August 2014. On the same date, other digital media such as the ‘noticias.lainformacion.com’ website also included the news item.

    On 7 August 2012, the online newspaper ‘finanzas.com’ revealed that the traditional gastronomy competition would use 150 kg of the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’.

    The fact that the ‘cortesano’ (befitting a royal court) chickpea of Valencia del Ventoso would be the main ingredient of a gastronomy competition was the subject of a press release on 7 August 2012 in the online media www.europapress.es and www.extremadura.com.

    The recent use of the name ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ is evident in the numerous articles and news items published by Hoy, Extremadura’s most widely circulated newspaper. Here are just a few examples:

    —

    Sunday, 9 December 1990: ‘“Garbanzos de Valencia del Ventoso” en la corte de Versalles’ [The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ at the Court of Versailles], by Lucio Poves.

    —

    Sunday, 18 August 2013: in an article referring to the ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ festival, the recipe expressly states: ‘Stew made with the “Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso”...’

    —

    Wednesday, 19 February 2025: Article by J.R. Alonso de la Torre with the subheading ‘Los “Garbanzos de Valencia del Ventoso” explican Extremadura’ [The ‘Garbanzo de Valencia del Ventoso’ represents Extremadura].
