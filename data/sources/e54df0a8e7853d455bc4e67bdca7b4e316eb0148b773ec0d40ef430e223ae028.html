Renters’ Rights Act: steps you need to take

5 May 2026

Most of the changes taking place in England under the Renters’ Rights Act have already been introduced in Wales in some form. However, some changes do apply, and landlords need to act to remain compliant.

What’s changing in Wales?

The main change coming in Wales is that from 1 June 2026, it will become unlawful for a landlord – or anyone acting on their behalf – to discriminate against applicants because they have children or receive benefits.

This means landlords and agents cannot refuse a prospective tenant from:

  • asking about or viewing a property
  • accessing information about the property
  • entering into an occupation contract
  • receiving benefits during occupation
  • having a child live at or visit the dwelling

What do landlords need to do?

A written statement must be given to the contract-holder no later than 14 days after the commencement of this new legislation on 1 June 2026 (so no later than 14 June 2026).

For new contracts

From 1 June 2026, all new occupation contracts should be issued with the new fundamental terms banning rental discrimination.

For existing contracts

For existing occupation contracts, landlords can either:

  • update the existing written statement of the occupation contract to incorporate the new fundamental terms and give this to the contract-holder, or
  • give a written statement of these new terms to their contract-holder and make clear where these new terms are to sit within the existing written statement

Support

Welsh Government has published guidance for landlords with an example of a written notice of variation.

Updated model written statements of occupation contracts with the new fundamental terms will be issued by Welsh Government on 1 June 2026.

Rent Smart Wales has launched a new Renters’ Rights Act page that explains what measures apply in Wales and the action you need to take.

Rent Smart Wales is unable to provide tailored advice about the Renters' Rights Act. Further information can be found on the Welsh Government website and assistance is also available from landlord associations and agent professional bodies.
